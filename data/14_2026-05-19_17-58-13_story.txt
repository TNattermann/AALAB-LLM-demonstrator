Berlin --  On the second day of the re:publica conference, focusing on digital topics, the conference participants discussed digital ethics and the future of technology in society. Unexpectedly, an extraordinary event captivated attendees and streamed across social media: renowned tech activist Clara Holm took the stage unannounced, bringing with her the prototype of a groundbreaking device designed to revolutionize personal data control.

Dubbed the "Personal Data Guardian" (PDG), this handheld gadget not only allows users to manage their online privacy but also empowers them to own and monetize their data. Clara showcased how the PDG operates in real-time, scanning users' devices and alerting them to data collection by third-party applications. Her demo featured an engaging visual representation of data flows, rallying crowds around the importance of digital empowerment in a world dominated by surveillance capitalism.

The atmosphere in the conference hall turned electric as Clara urged participants to take charge of their digital identities and push back against the prevailing narrative that personal data must be freely relinquished to tech giants.

Industry leaders, NGOs, and concerned citizens were quick to engage in spirited discussions following the presentation. Influential figures such as digital rights attorney Marc Hohlt and activist Lena Fischer joined Clara on stage for a panel that sparked intense debate on the implications of data ownership and the ethics of current digital practices. Audience members were encouraged to voice their opinions, creating a vibrant dialogue around the power dynamics shaped by technology today.

As Clara signed autographs for eager fans and engaged with participants, the buzz around the Personal Data Guardian intensified, prompting discussions of pre-orders. The re:publica conference closed its day acknowledging a landmark moment in the digital rights movement—a moment where innovation and activism collided to inspire a new chapter in personal data control. 

Re:publica has left attendees not just reflecting on the future of technology but emboldened to reclaim their rights in the digital age.
